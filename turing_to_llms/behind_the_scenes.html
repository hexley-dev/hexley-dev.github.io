The Story
01 — The Vision

One series. Ten issues.
Ninety years of computing.

From Turing to Swarms is a 10-issue comic series that tells the story of computing from Alan Turing's 1936 thought experiment to modern AI swarm intelligence — entirely produced by a constellation of specialized AI agents.

The production pipeline mirrors real film studios: a Researcher gathers historical facts, a Writer crafts narratives, an Editor and Red Team challenge the script, a Layout Designer composes panels, and an Image Generator renders the art. Every issue passes through multiple quality gates before publication.

05 — The Regen Rounds

75 issues tracked.
Six rounds of fixes.

AI image generation is probabilistic. Laptops appear in 1936 Cambridge. German text gets garbled. CRT monitors show up in the 1940s. Each problem was tracked, categorized, and systematically fixed.

By category:

Image Regen (34) SVG Fix (16) Layout Tweak (10) Prompt Fix (8) Manifest Edit (4) System Rule (3)

By resolution:

Fixed (43) Open (31) Won't Fix (1)
Round 1 — Initial Generation
438 images across 10 issues
First complete pass. ~80 minutes per issue on NVIDIA GB10. 52 problems identified in post-generation review.
Round 2 — Critical Fixes
4 critical + 8 warning issues
Laptops in 1930s scenes. Bell Labs sign text garbled. Terminal count mismatches (6 vs 5).
Round 3 — Prompt Refinement
7 panels re-prompted
Seed-then-prompt escalation: try 2-3 seeds first. If all fail, the problem is the prompt.
Round 4 — Text Errors
9 panels with garbled text
AI-generated text in non-English languages is notoriously unreliable. German umlauts, substitution errors.
Round 5 — Charts & Diagrams
3 visualization fixes
Complex diagrams moved to inline SVG. AI generation works for atmosphere, not technical accuracy.
Round 6 — Final Polish
5 panels for final pass
Dennis Ritchie portrait refinement. Last anachronism sweeps. V1+V2 shipped to hexley.dev.

The Frontier
06 — The Photo-Real Pivot

What if the robot
looked real?

With FLUX.2-dev (NVFP4 quantization) running on DGX Spark, we explored a new direction: photo-realistic Scribble in cinematic environments.

The likeness problem: Photo-realistic humans look great with FLUX.2-dev, but they drift from the actual historical figures. Alan Turing rendered photo-realistically looks like a convincing person — just not like Turing. A brass robot has no "real" reference to drift from — photo-realism makes it feel like a physical object you could hold.

❌ Photo-real Turing — doesn't look like him
✓ Watercolor Turing — safe
~ Hybrid — feels pasted

The solution: Scribble never shares scenes with human characters. Each panel type uses the style that works best for its subject. Photo-real brass robot. Ink-and-watercolor humans. No clash within any single image.

07 — Lessons Learned

What we learned
the hard way.

Lesson 01
One GPU process at a time
The initial image generation used Z-Image-Turbo locally. Two parallel runs crashed the GPU. The script that deleted 61 images before confirming torch worked was the costliest mistake. We now use FLUX.2-dev (NVFP4) on DGX Spark.
Lesson 02
Content before pixels
Lock the script with Editor + Red Team review before generating a single image. Content changes after image gen means throwing away expensive work.
Lesson 03
Seed-then-prompt escalation
Try 2-3 seeds. If all fail, the problem is the prompt, not the randomness. Mutate the prompt.
Lesson 04
Monospace everywhere in SVGs
Cursive and proportional fonts in SVG diagrams cause cramming and overlap. JetBrains Mono for ALL SVG text — no exceptions.
Lesson 05
Verify before walking away
"Running" ≠ "working." After launching any background batch, confirm the first 1-2 outputs exist and are valid.
Lesson 06
Photo-real humans → likeness drift
FLUX.2-dev generates convincing photo-real humans, but they drift from actual historical figures. Stylized illustration gives artistic license. Photo-real works beautifully for robots and environments.

Clean, modern, Apple-product energy. Minimal and precise. 62 images.
Style 5
Stylized 3D
3D-rendered characters in a hand-painted watercolor world — like Roger Rabbit inverted. 70 images.
Style 6
Full 3D
Everything rendered in 3D animated film style. Warm 1930s palette, cinematic depth of field. 8 images. (See side-by-side comparison with Stylized 3D.)
Style 7
Photo-Real Brass
Cinematic photo-realism with FLUX.2-dev. Robot feels tangible. Environments feel lived-in. 62 images.
Style 8
Graphic Novel Layout
Dense panel compositions — visual-first storytelling where images teach and text supports. 95 images.
