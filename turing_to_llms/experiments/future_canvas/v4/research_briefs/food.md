## GROUNDING BRIEF: Food & Agriculture

---

### 1. REAL PROJECTS

**[Plantix](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/programme/agritech/detecting-and-managing-crop-pests-and-diseases-with-ai-insights-from-plantix/)** — Built by PEAT GmbH in Berlin. A farmer photographs a sick leaf. The app — trained on 120 million crop images — names the disease, rates severity, and recommends treatment in seconds. Works offline. Covers 800 symptoms across 60 crop types. 30 million smallholder farmers across Africa and Asia use it today.

**[Tumaini](https://alliancebioversityciat.org/tools-innovations/tumaini-app-crop-health-bananas-beans)** — Built by CGIAR's Alliance of Bioversity International + CIAT. Specifically for bananas and beans — staple crops for subsistence farmers in East Africa and Latin America. Offline-capable. Active in Uganda, Kenya, DRC, Colombia, India. Detects whether that yellowing leaf is just a watering issue or the early signal of a fungal outbreak that would take out an entire season.

**[Winnow](https://www.closedlooppartners.com/how-ai-can-reduce-food-waste-at-restaurants/)** — A camera + AI system mounted above restaurant waste bins. It watches what gets thrown away, learns prep patterns, and tells the kitchen: "You prep 40 portions of salmon on Thursdays. You throw away 12." One restaurant cut food waste 30% in months. Used in hotel chains and large kitchens across Europe and Asia.

**[Too Good To Go](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/ai-in-the-food-industry)** — The consumer-facing version: an app connecting people with surplus cooked food from cafes and bakeries at end-of-day. Over 90 million users globally. A baker doesn't throw away tonight's unsold loaves — someone picks them up for $3 at 7pm.

**DIY Soil Sensors (Arduino/ESP32 community)** — Not a single company. A global maker movement. A $5 capacitive sensor + an ESP32 microcontroller + open-source firmware = a Wi-Fi-enabled soil monitor you build yourself. Active projects on GitHub, Arduino Project Hub, and Raspberry Pi forums. Small-scale farmers, urban gardeners, and students worldwide are building these for under $20.

### 2. REAL PEOPLE

A **52-year-old chili farmer in Maharashtra** who had never left his village. Saw yellowing leaves and curling stems. Called his son, who showed him Plantix. He photographed the leaf. The app said: *water stress, not disease*. He changed his irrigation schedule. His yield recovered. He told a reporter: "I saved Rs 1 lakh I would have spent on pesticides."

A **34-year-old extension worker in Uganda** covering 400 banana farms with no car and no budget for lab tests. She carries Tumaini on her phone. She walks the fields, photographs leaves, and gets a preliminary diagnosis before the lab could even receive a sample. Her advice to farmers is now faster and more specific.

A **27-year-old line cook in Stockholm** at a hotel restaurant. She watches the Winnow screen every Friday. It tracks what goes in the bin. She's the one who noticed the pattern — they over-prepped mushrooms every single week. She told her chef. They changed the order. Her observation, validated by the data, became a permanent menu change.

A **19-year-old agricultural student in Nairobi** who built a soil sensor for her small family plot using a YouTube tutorial, a $4 sensor, and an old phone. She's not a professional farmer. She did it to understand the soil. She now shares the data with her neighbors.

A **61-year-old baker in Dublin** who uses Too Good To Go every night. She doesn't think of herself as using AI. She thinks of it as "the app that sends people round at closing time." Her food waste bill dropped. A stranger got bread.

### 3. REAL MOMENTS

The **moment the diagnosis appears** — a farmer alone in a field, phone in hand, waiting two seconds for a result that used to require a specialist. The relief on his face when it says "manageable."

The **moment a season doesn't fail** — a farmer spots the disease in week one, not week six. The difference between a partial harvest and no harvest.

The **moment a cook changes a system** — the Winnow screen shows the pattern. The kitchen manager and the line cook look at the same data. A decision gets made. Waste stops.

The **moment a hobbyist's sensor works** — a graph on a phone shows live soil moisture. Just a number. But it's *her* number, from *her* ground, in real time. She's never had that before.

The **moment the app is passed between neighbors** — one farmer shows another on a cracked phone screen, under a tree, mid-afternoon. No WiFi, no agronomist, no middleman.

### 4. YOUR MOVE

**What it looks like:** A person at a kitchen table with a $15 Arduino kit, a soil sensor, and a laptop open to GitHub. A USB cable. A serial monitor showing numbers. They push the sensor into a pot of soil, watch the number change, and write their first line of code to log it. The screen fills with rows of data: timestamp, moisture percentage, temperature. They don't need a company. They did this in an afternoon. That's what "accessible to hobbyists now" actually looks like.
