## 1. REAL PROJECTS (Named, Active, Now)

**AEGIS — Perseverance's Rock-Hunting AI (NASA/JPL)**
Running on Mars since ~2022, now covering ~90% of the rover's drives autonomously. AEGIS identifies interesting rock formations in real-time and decides on its own to fire the laser spectrometer — no Earth sign-off needed. In December 2024, Perseverance completed its *first fully AI-planned drive*, co-developed with Anthropic's Claude. The rover drove 807 feet on Mars using a route no human had approved in advance.
[Source: NASA JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-perseverance-rover-completes-first-ai-planned-drive-on-mars/)

**FathomNet Portal (MBARI — Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute)**
An open database + AI tool launched at full capability in 2024. Scientists upload deep-sea video footage; the AI auto-classifies every creature in every frame. Without it, one ROV dive might generate 20 hours of video that takes months to label by hand. The Portal does it in minutes. Anyone — grad student, fisheries manager, curious biologist — can upload and process.
[Source: MBARI](https://annualreport.mbari.org/2024/story/inspiring-a-new-wave-of-ocean-explorers)

**Seabed 2030 + SeaDeep (Nippon Foundation / GEBCO)**
A global mission to map the entire ocean floor by 2030. As of 2024, only ~25% was mapped at usable resolution. A September 2024 partnership with SeaDeep deploys AI to stitch together fragments from thousands of research vessels worldwide — commercial ships, fishing boats, research cruises — transforming incidental depth readings into a unified map.
[Source: Seabed 2030](https://seabed2030.org/2024/09/17/seabed-2030-and-seadeep-join-forces-to-advance-ocean-exploration-through-ai/)

**Backyard Worlds: Cool Neighbors (NASA / Zooniverse)**
Citizens flip through infrared sky images looking for moving dots — objects too faint and cold to show up in standard surveys. AI pre-filters the data; humans catch what the AI misses. In early 2024, the project identified 89 new ultracool dwarf companion stars. Over 2.7 million registered Zooniverse volunteers contribute to projects like this.
[Source: Zooniverse / NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/citizen-science/backyard-worlds-cool-neighbors/)

**FathomVerse (MBARI — mobile game)**
Released as a standalone app in late 2024. Players swipe through real deep-sea footage on their phone, tagging creatures. Each swipe trains the AI. A retired postal worker in Ohio is, without knowing it, teaching a neural network to recognize a species of siphonophore that's never been seen before.
[Source: FathomNet](https://www.fathomnet.org/)

## 2. REAL PEOPLE

**The therapist who co-authored a paper**
Rosa Castro finished dinner, opened her laptop, and became one of four citizen scientists to co-author a paper in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* after identifying the first brown dwarf found by Backyard Worlds. No astronomy degree. No telescope. Just a laptop and pattern-recognition instinct that matched an AI's blind spot.

**The grad student in a coastal fisheries program**
A 27-year-old marine biology student at a mid-tier university in Chile. Her thesis depends on counting fish species in hours of ROV footage from Patagonian fjords. Three years ago, she'd have spent 18 months hand-labeling frames. With FathomNet, she spends two weeks on QA. The rest of her time: actual science.

**The JPL rover operator in Pasadena**
A 34-year-old software engineer who used to spend four hours every Martian morning routing Perseverance's path on a topographic map. Now she reviews AI-generated routes, catches edge cases, and sends approval. The shift freed her to work on the next-generation mission planner — the one that will work for Europa.

**The commercial fisherman off the Australian coast**
His boat's sonar pings are, without his knowledge, being collected by a Seabed 2030 partner vessel traveling the same waters. He's never heard of the project, but his incidental depth readings filled in a 40km gap on the ocean floor map — a canyon that turns out to be a critical habitat corridor for migratory sperm whales.

**The retired engineer playing FathomVerse**
65 years old, Tampa, Florida. He plays the ocean creature-tagging game during his morning coffee. In three months, he's tagged 4,000 images. One of his tags flagged a never-before-labeled specimen that a MBARI researcher noticed during a routine audit. He received an email. He cried.

## 3. REAL MOMENTS

**The rover drives itself for the first time**
The JPL team uploads the route parameters, hits send, and then... waits. It's 20 minutes before they even know if Perseverance received the command. When telemetry comes back fourteen hours later showing 807 feet traveled, a clean path, no stuck wheels, no emergency stops — someone in the operations center quietly says "it worked" to no one in particular.

## 4. YOUR MOVE

**On GitHub:** NASA open-sources everything. Search `github.com/nasa` — you'll find actual flight software, terrain analysis tools, the ML models that run on Perseverance. The repos have READMEs with photos of Mars. It looks like a normal GitHub page, except the issues are filed from Pasadena.

**On Zooniverse:** Go to `zooniverse.org`, click "Get Started." You'll land on a grid of active projects. Click *Galaxy Zoo: Weird & Wonderful* — within 30 seconds you're looking at a real galaxy image captured by a space telescope, and the UI asks you: "Does this look smooth and rounded, or does it have features?" You click. That click goes into a dataset. It looks like a quiz. It is, in fact, science.

**What it looks like:** You're sitting somewhere ordinary — a kitchen, a bus, a break room. On your screen is an actual image from 300 million light-years away, or from 1,200 meters below the ocean surface. There's a simple question. You know the answer. You click.
