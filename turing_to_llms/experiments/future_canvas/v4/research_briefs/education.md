### 1. REAL PROJECTS

**Khanmigo** (Khan Academy, USA — 700K+ students, 380+ school districts, 2024–25)
A Socratic AI tutor that refuses to give answers. Ask it "what's the answer?" and it asks you back: "What do you know so far?" Runs on GPT-4. Free for teachers. English Language Learner students showed a 0.31 standard deviation improvement in math — one of the largest measured gains in recent ed-tech research. Students who used it 30+ minutes/week moved from the 50th to the 59th percentile in math. [→ Khan Academy efficacy data](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/)

**RobotsMali** (Bamako, Mali — 300+ elementary students, 2023–24)
Mali's military government banned French as the national language in 2023. Problem: almost no written materials existed in Bambara, a language spoken by millions but rarely written. RobotsMali — a nonprofit backed by UNESCO, the Gates Foundation, and Google — used ChatGPT to generate children's stories, Google Translate to convert them to Bambara, and Playground AI to illustrate them. 107 books in under a year, at a fraction of traditional production cost. Most of these kids had never seen their language in printed form. [→ Washington Post coverage](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2024/04/13/mali-books-artificial-intelligence-ai/) · [→ Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/mali-ai-translate-local-language-education/)

**Code Club / Raspberry Pi Foundation** (global, 160+ countries)
Free after-school coding clubs for ages 9–13. In 2025, they released a full AI literacy curriculum: unplugged activities, animations, discussion prompts. No screen required for some lessons. Designed so a volunteer with no CS background can run it in a library or community center. [→ codeclub.org AI curriculum](https://codeclub.org/2025/02/26/get-your-code-club-coding-with-ai-and-ml/)

**AiAfrica Project** (54 countries across Africa, launched 2024)
Has trained 250,000 Africans in AI skills. Target: 11 million by 2028. Courses run from mobile phone-accessible platforms. Designed for people who can't access university, not those who can.

**AutoTutor** (research → deployed systems)
An intelligent tutoring system that watches how you answer — hesitations, vocabulary, confidence — and detects when you're frustrated versus when you're bored. Then it adapts: more scaffolding when you're lost, harder material when you're coasting. 87% of users received dynamically tailored content in studies.

### 4. YOUR MOVE

**Build a quiz bot. Volunteer for Code Club.**

It looks like this: You open a text editor. You write a question. You write three wrong answers and one right one. You write the thing it says when you get it wrong — something kind, specific, useful. Then you write the next question. After twenty questions you notice you've been thinking about *how people learn*, not just *what to teach*. That's learning design. You've done it.

Or: you show up at a library on a Wednesday. Nine kids look up. You say, "Let's make something move on the screen." You don't need to know everything. You need to be one lesson ahead.
