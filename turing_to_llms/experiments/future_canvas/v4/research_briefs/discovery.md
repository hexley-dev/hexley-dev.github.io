## 1. REAL PROJECTS (happening now)

**AlphaFold 3 — Google DeepMind**
Predicts the 3D shape of proteins and how they interact with DNA, RNA, and drug molecules. Before AlphaFold, solving one protein structure took years and millions in equipment. Now it takes minutes on a laptop. Over **3 million researchers in 190 countries** have used it — for malaria vaccines, cancer treatments, antibiotic resistance. Freely available at alphafold.ebi.ac.uk. Won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024.

**iNaturalist — Citizen Biodiversity Network**
A phone app that identifies any plant, animal, or fungus from a photo using AI computer vision trained on 300 million observations. You photograph a beetle on a park bench; the AI names the species; the sighting feeds into global biodiversity databases used by the IUCN to track endangered species. **3.3 million ordinary observers** worldwide. Free.

**SETI / Allen Telescope Array — Real-Time Fast Radio Burst Detection**
Astronomers built an AI (running on NVIDIA Holoscan) that processes radio telescope data **600 times faster** than before — fast enough to catch Fast Radio Bursts *as they happen*, not hours later when they're gone. First real-time AI search for FRBs in history, 2024.

**miniSASS — Stream Water Quality, Southern Africa**
In 2024, 1,000+ citizens in Southern Africa photographed organisms in stream water samples with a phone app. The AI identified the creatures and computed water quality from which species were present. No lab equipment. No chemistry degree. Just a phone and a stream.

**Insilico Medicine — ISM001-055**
An AI designed a drug candidate for a fatal lung disease (idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis) — from target identification to clinical trial — in **18 months for under $2.6 million**. Traditional drug discovery takes 10–15 years and costs $1–2 billion. Phase IIa trials showed measurable improvement in lung function.

## 3. REAL MOMENTS

**The protein folding.** A researcher types in a sequence — letters on a screen, meaningless-looking. Presses generate. Thirty seconds. A 3D ribbon of color appears and rotates. She stares. It's the shape of a protein no human has ever seen before. She screenshots it and texts her advisor at 11pm.

He looks around — he's in a garden in Malaysia. He didn't know this was remarkable. He submits the observation. It gets added to the global map.

**The water test.** The technician kneels at the creek bank. Photographs the stone. Waits. The app says: Clean. She exhales. Last month the reading said Polluted. Someone listened. The water changed. She doesn't know who fixed it. But she knows her data mattered.
