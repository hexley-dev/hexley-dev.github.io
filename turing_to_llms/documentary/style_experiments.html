The Frontier
06 — The Photo-Real Pivot

What if the robot
looked real?

With FLUX.2-dev (NVFP4) running on DGX Spark, we explored a new direction: photo-realistic Scribble in cinematic environments.

The likeness problem: Photo-realistic humans look great with FLUX.2-dev, but they drift from the actual historical figures. Alan Turing rendered photo-realistically looks like a convincing person — just not like Turing. A brass robot has no "real" reference to drift from — photo-realism makes it feel like a physical object you could hold.

❌ Photo-real Turing — doesn't look like him
✓ Watercolor Turing — safe
~ Hybrid — feels pasted

The solution: Scribble never shares scenes with human characters. Each panel type uses the style that works best for its subject. Photo-real brass robot. Ink-and-watercolor humans. No clash within any single image.

Issue 1 is our experimental proving ground. We've rendered the same story in eight completely different visual styles to find the right direction for the series. Every experiment below is a fully assembled, browseable comic.

Warm editorial illustration with ink lines and watercolor washes. The style that started the experiment series — 55 images.

Style 3
Frosted Glass
Ethereal, cool translucency. Beautiful but perhaps too cold for a warmly educational series. 62 images.
Style 4
White Matte
Clean, modern, Apple-product energy. Minimal and precise. 62 images.
Style 5
Stylized 3D
3D-rendered characters in a hand-painted watercolor world — the live-action/animation hybrid inverted. 70 images.
Style 6
Full 3D vs Stylized 3D
Everything rendered in a 3D animated style. Warm 1930s palette, cinematic depth of field. 8 images. Side-by-side comparison of both 3D approaches.
Style 7
Photo-Real Brass
Cinematic photo-realism with FLUX.2-dev. Robot feels tangible. Environments feel lived-in. 62 images.
Style 8

Dense panel compositions — visual-first storytelling where images teach and text supports. 95 images.
